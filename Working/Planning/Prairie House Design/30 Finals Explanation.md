# CORE DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Finals should test three things simultaneously:

1. **Decision-making without prompts**
    
2. **Consistency across environments**
    
3. **Integrity when no one is visibly watching**
    

---

# THE PRAIRIE HOUSE FINALS (STRUCTURE)

## PHASE 1 — CONTROLLED RELEASE (Days 1–5)

Rules loosen slightly:

- fewer verbal instructions
    
- broader time windows
    
- more independent task sequencing
    

But:

- outcomes still tracked
    
- errors still recorded
    

### Test:

> Can they operate without being told _when_ and _how_ every step happens?

---

## PHASE 2 — EXTERNAL EXPOSURE (Days 6–10)

Residents are sent into:

- diner shifts
    
- errands
    
- supervised off-site tasks
    

Alone or in pairs.

No staff present.

### Test:

> Do they behave the same way outside as inside?

---

## PHASE 3 — PRESSURE EVENTS (Days 11–14)

Introduce controlled disruptions:

- schedule changes without warning
    
- conflicting instructions
    
- delayed responses from staff
    
- ambiguous expectations
    

### Test:

> Do they default to discipline—or revert to instinct?

---

## PHASE 4 — SILENCE (Days 15–18)

This is the most important one.

- minimal interaction from staff
    
- no feedback
    
- no correction unless necessary
    

Residents must:

- maintain schedule
    
- self-correct
    
- hold standards without reinforcement
    

### Test:

> Who are you when no one is correcting you?

---

## PHASE 5 — THE EDGE TEST (Final 48 Hours)

This is your “red line” moment—but more subtle.

Give them:

- increased freedom window
    
- access to choices (time, movement, interaction)
    

And one quiet condition:

> **no one reminds them of the rules**
