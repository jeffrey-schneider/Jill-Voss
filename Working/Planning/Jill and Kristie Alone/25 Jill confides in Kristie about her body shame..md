Jill looked at Kristie differently now.

Not just emotionally.

Actually looked at her.

The purple hair had faded slightly over time, softer now around the edges, but under the patio lights it still carried deep streaks of color through the steam.

And the yellow swimsuit.

Jill had noticed it earlier without really processing it.

Now she did.

The yellow against the purple worked absurdly well together. Warm and bright against the cool darkness around the tub.

Kristie caught her staring.

“What?”

Jill shook her head once slowly.

“Nothing.”

But it wasn’t nothing.

Not tonight.

Tonight Jill’s mind kept drifting backward in strange directions.

County intake.  
Prairie View intake.  
Strip rooms.  
Commands.  
Shower lines.  
Women standing under fluorescent lights pretending not to see each other.

Jill had hated every second of it.

Not just because it was humiliating.

Because being seen had always made something inside her tighten.

Even before jail.

She thought suddenly about one of her aunts growing up. The way the woman’s eyes lingered too long around the girls in the family. Not touching. Never crossing any line anyone could clearly explain. But enough to make Jill spend years instinctively covering herself.

Enough to make nakedness feel unsafe long before jail ever weaponized it institutionally.

Kristie shifted closer slightly.

“You disappeared again.”

Jill blinked once.

“Sorry.”

Kristie smiled softly.

“You’ve done that a lot tonight.”

“Occupational hazard.”

Kristie laughed quietly at that.

Then Jill looked at her again.

Really looked.

The purple hair damp against her shoulders.  
The yellow suit bright beneath the steam.  
The easy way Kristie occupied her own body now.

Not performing.  
Not hiding.

Just existing.

And Jill realized with some embarrassment that she admired it.

A lot.

“I love your hair,” Jill said quietly.

Kristie blinked.

“What?”

“The purple.” Jill smiled faintly. “Against the yellow swimsuit? It is rocking.”

Kristie stared at her for half a second.

Then laughed.

A real laugh this time.

“You are such a lawyer.”

Jill frowned automatically.

“What does that mean?”

“You sound like you’re submitting evidence to the court.”

That actually made Jill laugh too.

Small at first.

Then bigger.

The sound surprised both of them.

Kristie’s expression softened immediately watching it happen.

Jill shook her head once.

“No, seriously.” Her voice quieted again. “You look…” She searched for the word carefully. “Comfortable.”

Kristie’s face changed slightly at that.

“Comfortable?”

“In your own skin.”

The hot tub jets hummed quietly beneath the water.

Kristie leaned back slowly.

“That took a while.”

Jill nodded.

“I think I’m just starting to understand that.”

Kristie looked at her carefully then.

Not flirtatious now.

Gentle.

“You know what Stephanie told me once?”

“What?”

“She said confidence isn’t thinking your body is perfect.” Kristie smiled faintly. “It’s learning your body isn’t a crime scene.”

That one hit Jill hard enough she actually looked away.

Because jail had absolutely made her body feel like evidence.  
Something processed.  
Observed.  
Managed.  
Controlled.

And maybe, if she was honest, she had felt that way long before incarceration ever reinforced it.

Kristie reached over slowly and touched Jill’s wrist beneath the water.

“You know what I see when I look at you?”

Jill hesitated.

“What?”

“A woman who survived.”

The answer landed differently than Jill expected.

Not desire.  
Not pity.

Recognition.

And sitting there beneath the Oklahoma night sky with steam drifting around them and Stephanie sleeping safely inside the house, Jill felt something unfamiliar begin loosening quietly inside her.

Not shame disappearing.

But maybe, finally, beginning to lose authority.
