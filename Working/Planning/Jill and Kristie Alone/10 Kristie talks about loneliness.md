Not with desire.

Not even with curiosity anymore.

Real fear.

“What?” she asked softly.

“You survived this before Stephanie.”

Kristie stayed quiet.

“Some days I didn’t handle it.”

Kristie’s voice stayed calm.

“That’s the honest answer.”

“There were nights I slept behind stores because the dumpsters blocked the wind.” she said. “I learned which places stayed warm longest after closing. Laundromats were good in winter if you had enough quarters to pretend you belonged there.”

Jill listened without moving.

“No one looks at homeless women for very long,” Kristie continued quietly. “That sounds safer than it really is.”

Jill understood immediately.

Too immediately.

Kristie noticed that too.

“You learn how to stay small,” Kristie said. “You learn how not to make eye contact. You learn how to move before someone decides you’re worth noticing.”

“Were you alone?”

“Jesus Christ.”

“Yeah.”

“The hardest part wasn’t hunger though.”

No jail.

Permanence.
