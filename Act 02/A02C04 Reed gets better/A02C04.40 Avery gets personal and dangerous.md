“Voss, Jill Margaret. Interview Room Two.”

The intercom clicked off.

Jill froze for half a second at the education table.

Grant looked up immediately.

“You okay?”

“Probably routine,” Jill answered automatically.

But her stomach had already tightened.

Yesterday had gone well.

Too well.

That was the problem.

Jill closed the GED workbook carefully and stood. The walk down the corridor felt heavier this time. By now she understood the rhythm of the place enough to know improvement rarely meant rest.

It usually meant the next layer.

When she reached Interview Room Two, Avery was already seated inside with several open folders spread neatly across the table.

More paperwork than usual.

That was new.

“Sit down, Voss.”

No good morning.

No warm-up.

Jill sat carefully.

Avery did not look up immediately. She turned one page slowly, then another.

Finally:

“Who is Stephanie Hammond?”

The question hit so hard Jill almost failed to answer.

“…what?”

Avery raised her eyes calmly.

“Stephanie Hammond.”

Jill stared at her.

The white-noise machine hummed softly near the wall.

Avery continued in the same professional tone she used discussing housing assignments or GED notes.

“Was she your lover?”

Jill felt heat rise immediately into her face.

“That’s personal.”

“Yes,” Avery agreed calmly. “It is.”

Silence.

Avery waited.

Jill’s pulse had already accelerated.

“Yes,” she answered finally.

Avery made a small notation.

“Did you cheat on her?”

Jill looked down sharply.

“No.”

Another note.

“Did you push her away?”

The room suddenly felt airless.

“I…”

Avery remained perfectly still.

“Or did she run?”

Jill swallowed hard.

“It wasn’t like that.”

Avery turned another page in the folder.

“Who is Lenore?”

Jill’s stomach dropped completely now.

The counselor already knew.  
Not fishing.  
Not guessing.

Knew.

Avery’s voice never changed.

“Was Lenore your lover?”

“Yes.”

“Concurrent with Stephanie Hammond?”

Jill stared at the table.

“Yes.”

The pen moved again across paper.

“Why?”

Jill’s breathing had become shallow enough now that she noticed it herself.

“I don’t know.”

“That answer is statistically unlikely.”

Jill looked up sharply.

Avery met her gaze evenly.

“You are a trained attorney. Your cognitive avoidance strategies are sophisticated but not invisible.”

The sentence landed like a hammer.

Avery folded her hands calmly.

“Why did you choose Lenore over Stephanie Hammond?”

“I didn’t.”

“Then why did Stephanie Hammond leave your life?”

Jill’s throat tightened.

“She deserved better.”

Avery tilted her head slightly.

“That is guilt. Not explanation.”

Silence again.

Jill could feel herself starting to lose emotional footing for the first time since arriving at PVH.

Not because Avery was cruel.

Because she was precise.

Avery continued quietly.

“Do you know what interests me about this pattern?”

Jill said nothing.

“You move toward people who need you.”

Another page turned.

“Reed.”

“Grant.”

“Even here.”

Avery watched her carefully now.

“But when someone begins needing you emotionally at the same level you need them…”

A pause.

“You retreat.”

Jill felt suddenly exposed in a way far worse than the medical exam had ever managed.

“That’s not true.”

Avery nodded once.

“Good.”

The answer startled Jill.

“Defend it.”

Silence.

Jill stared at her.

Avery’s voice remained perfectly level.

“If it’s false, explain why it’s false.”

Jill opened her mouth.

Stopped.

Because suddenly she realized Avery was not merely trying to hurt her.

She was forcing Jill to construct an identity stronger than shame.

And that was much harder.

Avery leaned back slightly.

“You’re entering your third week, Voss.”

Not accusation.

Assessment.

“The institution starts becoming survivable around now.”

Another pause.

“So this is where we find out whether you intend to become honest with yourself.”

The room stayed completely silent after that except for Jill’s uneven breathing and the faint hum of the white-noise machine near the wall.
