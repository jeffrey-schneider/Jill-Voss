Avery let the silence sit for several seconds after the last question.

Jill stared down at the table breathing carefully, trying to regain emotional control.

Then Avery spoke again.

And the direction changed completely.

“Here’s what I don’t understand, Voss.”

Jill looked up warily.

Avery’s expression remained calm. Analytical.

“You claim you loved Stephanie Hammond deeply.”

“Yes.”

“You describe her as important enough to destroy yourself over.”

Jill’s chest tightened immediately.

Avery leaned slightly forward.

“Then why did you let her go?”

The question landed harder than the others somehow.

Jill blinked.

“What?”

“If she mattered that much, why didn’t you fight for the relationship?”

Jill opened her mouth slightly.

No answer came.

Avery continued in the same measured tone.

“Why did you rush across Oklahoma trying to reach some party instead of acting like a functioning adult?”

Jill flinched visibly.

“You broke multiple traffic laws.”

Another sentence.

“You detonated your career.”

Another.

“You spent the last twenty-four months inside legal collapse.”

Avery’s eyes never left her.

“All because you were afraid of arriving late?”

Jill’s breathing had become shallow again.

Avery pressed cleanly into the opening.

“All you had to do was call her.”

Silence.

“Tell her you’d be late.”

Another pause.

“Tell her to wait.”

The white-noise machine hummed softly behind them.

“You were supposedly racing toward the woman you loved,” Avery said quietly, “but you never performed the one emotionally vulnerable action that actually mattered.”

Jill stared at her.

Avery’s voice stayed perfectly level.

“You didn’t trust that she would still want you if you disappointed her.”

The words hit with surgical precision.

Jill looked away sharply.

“That’s not true.”

“No?”

Avery waited.

“Then why didn’t you call?”

Silence.

Longer this time.

Jill’s throat worked once before she finally answered.

“Because if she sounded disappointed…”

Her voice broke slightly.

“…I don’t think I could’ve handled it.”

The room went completely still.

Avery did not move.

Did not rescue.

Did not soften.

She simply let Jill hear what she had finally said aloud.

After several seconds Avery spoke quietly.

“So instead you chose control.”

Jill closed her eyes briefly.

“You drove faster.”

Another pause.

“You tried to outrun shame.”

The sentence settled into the room like weight.

Jill sat motionless.

Avery leaned back slightly in the chair.

“That’s the tragedy here, Voss.”

Jill looked up slowly.

“You didn’t destroy your life because you loved Stephanie Hammond too much.”

The counselor folded her hands calmly atop the folder.

“You destroyed it because you were too frightened to risk hearing that she might still love you imperfectly.”

Tears finally escaped before Jill could stop them.

Not dramatic.

Just exhausted.

Avery handed her the tissue box without comment.

Then:

“That,” Avery said quietly, “is a problem worth fixing.”

And for the first time in the entire conversation, Jill did not feel attacked.

She felt seen.
