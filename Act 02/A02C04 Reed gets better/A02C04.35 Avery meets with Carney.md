Carney’s office door remained open when Avery arrived the next morning.

The administrative wing had already settled into weekday rhythm. Phones ringing. Movement sheets changing hands. Radios crackling softly from the security desk farther down the corridor.

Doctor Tim Carney sat behind the desk reviewing overnight reports with a yellow legal pad beside one elbow.

He looked up as Avery entered.

“What’s new?”

Avery closed the door halfway behind her and took the chair opposite the desk.

“Voss.”

That got his attention immediately.

Carney set one folder aside.

“What about her?”

Avery opened the counseling file.

“She’s stabilizing faster than expected.”

Carney leaned back slightly.

“Externally or internally?”

“Both, I think.”

Avery turned one page.

“She’s beginning to function without waiting for certainty first.”

“That’s improvement.”

“Yes sir.”

Carney nodded once for her to continue.

Avery glanced briefly at her notes.

“She engaged Reed voluntarily. Reed’s social withdrawal decreased almost immediately afterward.”

“I saw Martin’s briefing note.”

Avery continued.

“Now she’s attached herself to Grant’s GED work.”

Carney’s expression shifted faintly.

“The English problem?”

“Yes sir.”

“She helping or controlling?”

“That’s what I tested yesterday.”

Carney waited.

Avery folded her hands over the file.

“She initially framed her involvement as obligation. Said I told her to help.”

Carney almost smiled.

“But you didn’t.”

“No sir.”

“What happened when you pushed it?”

Avery looked mildly satisfied now.

“She defended the decision morally instead of procedurally.”

That interested him.

“How?”

Avery glanced briefly at the notation before quoting it almost exactly.

> “Because nobody should feel stupid for trying.”

Carney stayed quiet for a second after hearing that.

Then:  
“That’s genuine.”

“Yes sir.”

Avery nodded once.

“She’s moving away from pure compliance behavior.”

Carney tapped his pen lightly once against the desk.

“Good.”

Avery continued:  
“She still catastrophizes consequences internally. But she’s acting despite the fear now instead of freezing under it.”

Carney considered that carefully.

“That’s closer to real adaptation.”

“Yes sir.”

The office remained quiet for a moment except for distant hallway movement outside the partially open door.

Finally Carney asked:  
“How’s Grant responding?”

“Better.”

Avery turned another page.

“Grant completed two written exercises voluntarily yesterday without shutdown behavior.”

“That fast?”

“She trusts Voss.”

Carney’s eyes narrowed slightly.

“Monitor dependency.”

“Already am.”

Avery closed the folder partway.

“I think Voss functions best when given responsibility connected to another human being.”

Carney nodded slowly.

“That tracks.”

Avery studied him briefly.

“You already thought that.”

“I suspected it.”

Carney stood and walked toward the office window overlooking the interior yard.

Outside, offenders in green work uniforms moved landscaping tools across the gravel pathways under supervision.

“Some people adapt by submitting,” he said calmly.

Avery remained silent.

“Others adapt by becoming useful.”

He turned back toward her.

“Which one is Voss?”

Avery answered without hesitation.

“Useful.”

Carney nodded once.

“Good.”

Then after a pause:

“Useful people usually survive.”

The statement did not sound comforting.

Just observational.

Carney returned to the desk and picked up the next folder.

“Keep pressure on her.”

“Yes sir.”

“But not enough to collapse the structure you’re building.”

Avery stood.

“Yes sir.”

As she reached the door Carney spoke once more without looking up from the paperwork.

“And Avery?”

“Yes sir?”

“Outstanding work.”

Avery allowed herself the faintest trace of a smile before stepping back into the corridor.
