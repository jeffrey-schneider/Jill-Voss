Breakfast movement formed slowly beneath the harsh morning lights.

Rubber sandals scraped across concrete while Pod Two assembled into chow line order with trays tucked beneath their arms. The room still carried that half-awake silence unique to early institutional mornings.

Jill stood midway through the queue waiting for the serving line to move.

For the first few minutes nobody spoke to her.

Then someone stepped quietly into position beside her.

Marla Reed.

Her regulation haircut still sat unevenly around her face from the previous afternoon. The shorter hair exposed tiredness she could no longer hide behind.

She kept her eyes forward.

“So,” Marla said quietly, “a horse walks into a bar.”

Jill glanced sideways.

“The bartender says, ‘What’ll you have… and why the long face?’”

A pause.

“Get it?”

Jill stared at her for one second before the joke connected.

A small laugh escaped her unexpectedly.

It sounded strange in the breakfast line. Almost unfamiliar.

Marla’s mouth twitched slightly upward.

“It’s awful,” Jill whispered.

“I know.”

The line advanced another step.

Around them trays clattered softly while kitchen workers spooned powdered eggs onto segmented trays beneath the supervision window.

Marla leaned slightly closer.

“I spent like twenty minutes trying to remember a joke last night.”

“Why?”

Marla shrugged faintly.

“You said you were lonely.”

Jill looked down briefly at the tray in her hands before the line moved again.

Something inside her chest eased for the first time since arriving.

Not safety.

Just less isolation.

Near the officer station Deputy Martin reviewed the breakfast movement sheet while quietly observing Pod Two.

Reed was standing upright.  
Engaging voluntarily.  
Eating.  
Initiating social contact.

Better.

Much better.

Martin mentally added the observation to morning briefing before she even finished count verification.

Not because counseling required optimism.

Because PVH tracked stabilization indicators as carefully as discipline problems.

Who isolated.  
Who integrated.  
Who resisted.  
Who bonded.  
Who emotionally flattened.  
Who started functioning again.

Negative behavior mattered.

But so did recovery.

And this morning, for the first time in days, Marla Reed looked like somebody participating in the room instead of disappearing from it.
