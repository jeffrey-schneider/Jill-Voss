Avery let Jill cry for a little while.

Not long.

Just enough for the emotional wall to crack instead of shatter.

The tissue box remained between them on the table. Jill sat leaning slightly forward now, shoulders tighter than before, eyes reddened, trying to regain control of herself with visible effort.

Avery waited until Jill’s breathing steadied somewhat.

Then she continued.

And somehow the next questions felt even harder.

“So,” Avery said quietly, “what do you have to do to get back into Stephanie Hammond’s life?”

Jill froze.

The question sounded impossible.

Avery remained calm.

“I assume that’s what you want.”

Jill stared down at the table.

“Maybe we should confirm it.”

Silence.

“Do you want back into Stephanie Hammond’s life?”

Jill’s throat tightened immediately.

“Yes.”

The answer came almost too fast.

Avery nodded once.

“Good.”

Then:

“Can you confirm that for me?”

Jill looked confused.

“I just did.”

“No.”

Avery’s tone remained level.

“You answered reflexively.”

The counselor leaned slightly forward.

“Can you confirm it consciously?”

The room stayed very quiet.

Jill wiped once at her face with the tissue still in her hand.

Finally:  
“Yes.”

Avery watched her carefully.

“Can you confirm it for yourself?”

That one hurt.

Jill looked away toward the concrete wall.

Because suddenly the question underneath the question became obvious.

Not:  
Do you love Stephanie?

But:  
Do you actually believe you deserve to return?

Jill closed her eyes briefly.

“Yes,” she whispered.

Avery nodded once.

Better.

Then the counselor turned the blade slightly.

“Have you dealt with the jealousy yet?”

Jill’s eyes opened immediately.

“What?”

Avery did not soften.

“Stephanie Hammond is living a life outside this facility.”

Another pause.

“She did not stop existing because you were incarcerated.”

Jill’s chest tightened again.

Avery continued carefully, clinically.

“She’s living with someone else now, isn’t she?”

Jill stayed silent.

“Kristie?”

Still silence.

“Someone sharing her home while you sleep in a concrete box.”

The words landed brutally because Avery delivered them without malice. Pure observation. Reality stated plainly.

Jill looked suddenly furious.

Not at Avery.

At the truth itself.

Avery saw it immediately.

“Good,” she said quietly.

Jill looked up sharply.

“You need access to that anger.”

The counselor folded her hands.

“Not because anger is healthy. Because denial is worse.”

Jill’s voice came out strained now.

“You think I don’t know she moved on?”

“I think you avoid emotionally visualizing it.”

That hit hard enough Jill physically recoiled again.

Avery pressed carefully.

“Have you imagined them together?”

Jill looked sick.

“No.”

“Why not?”

Because it would kill me.

The answer flashed through her mind instantly.

Avery watched her closely.

“There it is.”

Jill’s breathing had become uneven again.

Avery’s voice softened only slightly now.

“Voss, if you intend to re-enter Stephanie Hammond’s life someday, then eventually you will have to stop competing against fantasy versions of yourself.”

Silence.

“You lost years.”

Another pause.

“She didn’t.”

Jill lowered her head.

Avery continued:

“So the real question becomes this.”

The counselor leaned forward slightly.

“When you finally stand in front of Stephanie Hammond again…”

The room felt completely still.

“…will you be asking her to rescue you?”

A pause.

“Or will you be arriving as someone emotionally capable of standing beside her?”

Jill could not answer immediately.

Because for the first time since her arrest, she understood those were not remotely the same thing.

And Avery knew it.
