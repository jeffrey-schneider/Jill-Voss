Morning briefing had already moved past overnight counts and medical scheduling by the time Deputy Martin mentioned Pod Two.

The small conference room smelled faintly of coffee and dry erase markers. Shift supervisors sat along one side of the table while counseling and medical staff occupied the other. A whiteboard listed housing notes, movement changes, and follow-up observations in neat block print.

Counselor Avery sat near the end of the table reviewing her folder quietly.

Doctor Tim Carney stood near the board listening while flipping slowly through the overnight summaries.

“Reed showed improvement this morning,” Martin reported. “Voluntary engagement. Ate breakfast. Initiated peer interaction with Voss during chow line.”

Carney looked up immediately.

“Reed initiated?”

“Yes sir.”

Avery stopped writing.

Martin continued:  
“Minimal but noticeable behavioral shift from prior observation pattern.”

Carney closed the folder in his hands.

“What kind of interaction?”

Martin allowed herself the faintest trace of amusement.

“Apparently Reed spent twenty minutes remembering a horse joke.”

A couple people around the table smiled briefly.

Even Avery’s expression softened for half a second.

Carney nodded once.

“Humor returning.”

“Yes sir.”

He looked toward Avery.

“Avery, watch this blooming.”

The room quieted slightly.

“You and your team keep pressure balanced correctly and we may have something useful developing here.”

Avery nodded once.

“Yes sir.”

Carney tapped the folder lightly against the table edge while thinking.

“It’s early. Very early. But Reed re-engaging through peer stabilization instead of formal intervention is preferable.”

“Yes sir,” Avery agreed.

Carney shifted his attention back toward Martin.

“Who initiated contact between the two?”

Martin answered without hesitation.

“I did, sir.”

The room remained quiet.

Carney studied her for a moment.

“Why?”

Martin folded her hands behind her back.

“Seemed low risk. Reed was withdrawing harder every hour. Voss presented stable enough for limited peer engagement.”

Avery listened carefully without interrupting.

Martin continued:  
“I believed the interaction had a reasonable chance of achieving desired outcome.”

Carney nodded once.

“Correct assessment.”

Martin remained expressionless, though her shoulders eased slightly.

Carney pointed once with the folder in his hand.

“Don’t misunderstand me, Martin. Structure matters here. Documentation matters.”

“Yes sir.”

“But so does initiative.”

He set the folder down.

“You identified a low-risk intervention point and used discretion appropriately.”

Martin nodded once.

“Yes sir.”

Carney’s expression remained calm and professional.

“Keep making decisions like that without fear of failure and you have a future here.”

“Thank you sir.”

Avery finally spoke from the end of the table.

“Voss may respond well to controlled responsibility.”

Carney glanced toward her.

“I noticed.”

Avery opened Reed’s file again.

“She stabilizes when given purposeful human engagement. Reed appears to respond to being needed rather than being managed.”

Carney considered that quietly.

“Then monitor both carefully.”

His tone sharpened slightly.

“I want support. Not dependency.”

“Yes sir,” Avery answered immediately.

Carney picked the folder back up.

“Good work all around.”

Then, without changing tone at all:

“Now tell me why Pod Four missed shower timing by nine minutes.”

The meeting moved on.

But Avery quietly underlined Jill Voss’s name once before closing the file
