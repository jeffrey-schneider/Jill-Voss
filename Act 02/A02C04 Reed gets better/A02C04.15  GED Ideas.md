Deputy Martin was halfway through signing out when Counselor Avery caught up with her near the rear administrative corridor.

The morning shift exchange had already started. Radios crackled softly from the intake desk farther down the hall while incoming officers collected housing assignments from the duty board.

Avery stepped beside her.

“Outstanding work last night, Martin.”

Martin looked over slightly.

“Thank you ma’am.”

“The boss liked what you did.”

That carried weight coming from Avery. Carney did not hand out approval casually.

Martin slid her pen back into her pocket.

“Seemed like the pod needed less pressure for a few hours.”

Avery nodded once.

“Correct call.”

The counselor leaned lightly against the corridor wall, folder tucked beneath one arm.

“Any other ideas you’ve got?”

Martin hesitated only briefly.

“We’ve got one in Pod Two struggling with the English portion of GED prep.”

Avery listened immediately.

Martin continued:  
“Smart girl. Embarrassed about it. Shuts down during group instruction.”

Avery already looked interested.

“You think Voss could help?”

Martin shrugged faintly.

“Depends how educated she is.”

Then:  
“She speaks carefully. Organized thoughts. Watches wording. Probably writes well.”

Avery considered that quietly.

The idea fit almost too neatly with what she had already been seeing in Jill:

- high verbal control,
- strong procedural awareness,
- stabilizing behavior under structure,
- responsiveness to purposeful responsibility.

“Damn good idea,” Avery said softly.

Martin allowed herself the faintest trace of satisfaction.

Avery’s expression remained thoughtful now.

“If Voss teaches, she gains purpose. If the other offender improves, we gain engagement on both sides.”

“And if it fails?” Martin asked.

Avery smiled slightly.

“Then we adjust.”

Very calm.  
Very simple.

The counselor pushed away from the wall.

“Let’s explore it carefully before custody formalizes anything. I want it to feel natural inside the pod.”

Martin nodded.

“Yes ma’am.”

Avery started down the corridor toward counseling offices, then paused briefly.

“One more thing.”

Martin stopped.

“You noticed the difference between Reed last night and Reed this morning?”

“Yes ma’am.”

“That’s what hope looks like at this stage.”

Then Avery continued walking without another word.

Martin stood there quietly for a second before finishing shift sign-out
