The elevator ride down felt longer than she remembered.

Concrete walls.  
Fluorescent light.  
Belly chain tight against her waist.

The female deputy remained beside her while the male deputy handled paperwork downstairs.

No conversation.

Just movement.

The secure corridor opened toward intake.

And Jill saw it again.

The strip room door.

Heavy.  
Rectangular window.  
Bright light beyond it.

Her stomach tightened immediately.

The deputy beside her noticed.

“Long forty-five?” she asked quietly.

Jill answered honestly.

“Yes.”

The deputy nodded once.

“I’ve seen worse.”

Then the intake door buzzed open.

---

Everything repeated.

Property.  
Shoes.  
Paperwork.  
Standing.  
Waiting.

Her clothing disappeared into bins.

Then:  
strip search.

Procedural.  
Clinical.  
Familiar now in the worst possible way.

The green inmate uniform returned shortly afterward.

County cotton.  
Heavy.  
Institutional.

The deputy handed it across the counter.

“Dress out.”

Jill took it silently.

Forty-five days.

---

The days moved strangely after that.

Slow individually.  
Fast in clusters.

Jill learned quickly she was not alone.

Three other women in the jail were awaiting Prairie View intake.

Nobody discussed it openly much.

But everyone knew.

The women spoke quietly at night sometimes.

Rumors mostly.

Questions nobody could answer.

What was The Finals?  
Did people fail?  
What happened there?  
What was the place really like?

No one knew.

And the uncertainty became its own pressure.

---

Then came the waiting.

No dates.  
No schedules.  
No notice.

Only:  
“Transport will occur on the first.”

Nothing more.
