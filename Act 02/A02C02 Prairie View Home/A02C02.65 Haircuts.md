At 1245 hours the pod speaker cracked once with static.

“Pod Two. Lineup for scheduled movement.”

The room reacted immediately.

Books closed.  
Blankets folded back.  
Conversations ended mid-sentence.

The offenders moved toward the yellow line near the officer station in practiced silence. Black-and-white striped pajamas filled the center aisle of the pod. Every first monther wore the same soft cotton uniform, the same rubber sandals, the same striped cap pulled low over contained hair.

Jill adjusted hers automatically as she stepped into place.

Deputy Keller emerged from the officer station carrying a clipboard.

“Listen carefully. You will line up in the order names are called. Maintain spacing. No talking during movement. Hands visible at all times. You are being escorted to scheduled grooming appointments.”

Nobody answered.

Keller glanced down at the clipboard.

“Voss, Jill Margaret.”

Jill stepped forward first.

Every eye in the pod touched her for a second.

Not anger exactly.

Awareness.

“Front position,” Keller ordered.

“Yes sir.”

Jill moved to the head of the line near the pod door. The glass reflected her faintly back at herself. Black-and-white stripes. Cap pulled tight. Hands locked behind her back.

Keller continued reading names.

“Jonson, Cam.”

“Reed, Marla.”

“Hernandez, Tasha.”

The line slowly formed behind Jill in quiet shuffling movements and rubber soles against concrete.

No one complained.

That was over now.

The board had announced the appointment yesterday. Everyone had already lived through the anger stage. What remained was resignation mixed with curiosity about how much “close regulation” actually meant.

Keller finished the last name and looked up.

“Twelve present. Good.”

He stepped toward Jill.

“You understand why you are first in line, Voss?”

“Yes sir.”

“Good. Saves confusion.”

A few heads lowered behind her.

Keller pressed the control button beside the door. The magnetic lock released with a heavy metallic click.

“Movement.”

The line began walking.

Jill stepped through first.

The corridor outside the pod smelled faintly of disinfectant and floor wax. Fluorescent lights reflected off polished concrete block walls painted institutional beige halfway up from the floor.

Their sandals slapped softly against the corridor in uneven rhythm.

Two officers waited farther down the hall near the intersection.

One glanced at the approaching line and smirked slightly.

“Barber day?”

“Yes,” Keller answered calmly. “Pod Two.”

The officer looked directly at Jill at the front.

“Hell of a way to make friends your first week.”

Keller did not react.

“Keep moving.”

Jill kept her eyes forward.

The line turned left at the next corridor. A printed sign mounted beside the doorway ahead read:

GROOMING SERVICES

Below it, someone had taped another sheet:  
REGULATION CUTS ONLY

Keller stopped the line outside the door.

“Single file. You will enter when instructed. Caps off inside. Follow directions exactly.”

Jill could hear electric clippers buzzing faintly beyond the door.

One of the women farther back swallowed audibly.

Keller looked at Jill again.

“You wanted special attention, Voss. Congratulations.”

Then he opened the door.
