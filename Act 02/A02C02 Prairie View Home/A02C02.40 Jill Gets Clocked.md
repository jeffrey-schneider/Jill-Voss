Jill did not understand the caps at first.

They had been issued with the rest of the first-monther clothing during intake. Heavy black-and-white cotton pajamas. Matching drawstring pants. Thin rubber sandals. One soft striped cap.

“Cap stays on outside your bunk,” Deputy Sheridan had said during orientation. “Hair stays inside the cap. If your hair escapes the cap, fix it before staff fixes it for you.”

At the time, Jill thought the warning sounded exaggerated.

It did not take long to understand otherwise.

The first correction came during breakfast line the next morning.

The pod had shuffled into formation half asleep, moving toward the stainless counters while the television murmured weather somewhere overhead. Jill carried her tray forward with the others.

“Hold.”

The word cut through the line.

Deputy Keller stepped beside her without raising his voice. He pointed once toward the back of her head.

Jill reached automatically. Several strands had worked loose beneath the cap near the nape of her neck.

“Fix it.”

“Yes sir.”

“First monthers wear the cap for a reason.”

Jill tucked the hair back underneath. Keller watched for another second, then nodded once and moved on.

No lecture.  
No paperwork.  
No scene.

Just correction.

The second time happened during evening assembly.

The pod stood beside their tables while Sergeant Dovie Sheridan walked the row with a clipboard under one arm. Jill had started learning that Sheridan saw everything. Loose strings. Untucked shirts. Slow responses. Talking eyes.

Sheridan stopped directly in front of her.

“Remove your cap.”

Jill obeyed.

A section of hair near her left ear had slipped partially free and flattened against her cheek with sweat from the Oklahoma heat.

“How long has that been out?”

“I don’t know, ma’am.”

“That answer concerns me.”

“Yes ma’am.”

Sheridan held out her hand.

Jill gave her the cap.

“Hair containment is part of uniform compliance for first monthers. You have now been corrected twice in less than twenty-four hours.”

Sheridan wrote something on the clipboard.

The sound of the pen bothered Jill more than the words.

“Fix yourself.”

“Yes ma’am.”

When the cap was returned, Jill spent the rest of assembly painfully aware of every loose strand touching her skin.

The third time happened the next afternoon during corridor movement.

The facility had the dry heat that made everything feel dusty by midday. Jill had been sweating beneath the cap since recreation. A few strands escaped again near her collar as the pod returned from chow.

“Voss.”

She stopped immediately.

Officer Hernandez motioned her toward the wall.

“Face.”

Jill turned.

He adjusted the flashlight clipped to his vest and looked at the back of her cap for barely two seconds.

“You people really think staff won’t notice this?”

“No sir.”

“Apparently you do.”

He took out a small notepad.

Jill felt her stomach tighten.

“This is your third correction.”

“I’m sorry, sir.”

“That part comes after the haircut.”

He finished writing.

The notepad disappeared back into his pocket.

“Move.”

That evening Jill sat on the edge of her bunk after count and watched several of the other first monthers quietly retucking their own hair beneath their caps.

Nobody said anything to her.

Nobody needed to.
