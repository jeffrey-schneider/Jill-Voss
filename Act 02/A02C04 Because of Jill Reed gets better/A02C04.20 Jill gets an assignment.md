Counselor Avery caught Jill near the education board two days later during afternoon movement.

“Voss.”

Jill stopped immediately.

“Yes ma’am.”

Avery glanced briefly toward Pod Two where Marla Reed sat at one of the tables working through a workbook with another offender.

“Reed’s recovery has been fairly robust.”

Jill looked over automatically.

“Yes ma’am.”

Avery nodded once.

“Fun watching people grow when they decide to.”

Jill was not entirely sure how to answer that.

Avery shifted the folder beneath one arm.

“We’ve got another offender in the pod struggling with the English portion of GED prep.”

Jill listened carefully.

“Grant,” Avery continued. “Bright enough. Freezes when words get complicated.”

“Yes ma’am.”

Avery looked at her calmly.

“You’ve got some degree in something useless, correct? Religion? Grievance studies? Interpretive basket weaving?”

For one horrifying second Jill could not tell whether she was serious.

Then Avery’s mouth twitched very slightly at one corner.

The closest thing to humor Jill had yet seen from her.

“I’m an attorney, ma’am.”

“Ah.”

Avery nodded once.

“So the answer is yes. You’ve used English before.”

Jill nearly smiled despite herself.

Nearly.

Avery continued before the moment softened too far.

“You interested in helping her?”

The question landed differently because Avery phrased it as voluntary.

Not assignment.  
Not treatment plan.  
Not correctional duty.

Help.

Jill thought about the last several days:  
Reed,  
the pod,  
the white board,  
the feeling of becoming smaller inside the institution.

Then she looked back toward the tables.

“I think so, ma’am.”

Avery studied her for one quiet second.

“Good.”

She handed Jill a thin GED practice workbook from beneath the folder.

“Don’t do the work for her. Teach structure. Confidence first. Grammar second.”

“Yes ma’am.”

Avery started to leave, then paused.

“Oh, and Voss?”

“Yes ma’am?”

“If she asks whether semicolons matter in real life, lie to her.”

Then Avery continued down the corridor while Jill stood there holding the workbook like she had just been handed something far more dangerous than paperwork.

Purpose.
