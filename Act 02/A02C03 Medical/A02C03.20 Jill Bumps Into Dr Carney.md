Jill stepped out of Exam Room One still adjusting the tie at the back of the gown when she struck someone solid in the corridor.

Not hard.

Just enough to stop her short.

She looked up immediately.

Dr. Tim Carney.

He stood with a folder tucked beneath one arm speaking quietly with one of the physician assistants near the staging desk. Unlike custody staff, Carney did not wear a uniform. Dark slacks. White shirt. Facility identification clipped neatly at the belt.

Jill stepped backward at once.

“Excuse me, Doctor,” she said quickly. “I didn’t see you there.”

Carney looked at her for one silent second.

Not angry.

Assessing.

“You should pay closer attention to your surroundings, Voss.”

“Yes sir.”

The PA beside him lowered her eyes toward the clipboard in her hands, suddenly finding it very important.

Carney glanced once toward the chart still hanging outside Exam Room One.

“How was your visit?”

“Routine, sir.”

“Good.”

He said it like confirmation rather than concern.

Carney shifted slightly to allow the corridor movement to continue.

“You are still in processing status. Maintain gown control while moving through medical.”

Jill immediately pulled the loose side of the gown tighter against herself.

“Yes sir.”

Carney watched her fix it.

“Better.”

He turned back toward the PA without dismissing her verbally. The conversation simply resumed around her as though she had already ceased to matter operationally.

“Pod Three arrives tomorrow morning?” Carney asked.

“Yes sir. Eight hundred hours.”

“Ensure additional staging chairs are brought over before intake begins. Last cycle bottlenecked near post-processing.”

“Yes sir.”

Jill remained standing one second too long.

Carney looked at her again.

“Were you waiting for something, Voss?”

“No sir.”

“Then continue movement.”

“Yes sir.”

Jill moved quickly toward the post-processing corridor where the other first monthers waited for their return showers and replacement uniforms.

Behind her, Carney’s voice continued calmly discussing schedules, staffing, and throughput as though the entire medical operation were nothing more than another administrative system to optimize.

"Voss! Maintain gown control. " said Dr. Carney as he took out a pen to begin writing something down. Jill didn't understand what he had said and suddenly realized that her bare bottom was showing.

Jill grabbed the back of the gown closed immediately.

“I’m sorry, sir.”

Carney wrote for another few seconds before answering.

“You are responsible for maintaining issued attire at all times during movement.”

“Yes sir.”

“Medical processing does not suspend standards.”
