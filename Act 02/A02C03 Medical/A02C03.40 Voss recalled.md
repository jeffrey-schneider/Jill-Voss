The pod door opened again at 1158 hours.

Counselor Avery stepped back inside carrying her clipboard against one hip.

Most of Pod Two was already settling into pre-lunch routine. A few offenders looked up automatically when she entered.

Deputy Keller glanced over from the officer station.

“You done?”

“Almost.”

Avery scanned the pod once.

“Voss.”

Jill’s stomach tightened immediately.

“Yes ma’am.”

“Interview Room Two again, please.”

Several heads lifted this time.

That was worse.

Jill stood quietly and followed Avery from the pod without asking questions.

The second walk felt longer than the first.

When they reached Interview Room Two, Avery held the door open for her.

“Sit down.”

Jill obeyed.

This time Avery did not open the folder immediately. She sat across from Jill with both hands resting loosely atop the closed file.

The room stayed quiet for several seconds.

Finally Avery spoke.

“You’ve become significantly more careful since intake.”

Jill answered cautiously.

“Yes ma’am.”

“Most first monthers do.”

Avery tilted her head slightly.

“Some become more stable with structure.”

The sentence hung there.

“Others become harder to read.”

Jill said nothing.

Avery nodded faintly, almost as though silence itself had answered the question.

“You understand that every department here communicates with each other.”

“Yes ma’am.”

“Medical. Custody. Classification. Counseling.”

Avery tapped the closed folder lightly once.

“When patterns appear, we compare notes.”

Jill kept her posture straight even though she suddenly wanted very badly to move.

Avery noticed that too.

“You are trying very hard to present yourself correctly.”

“Yes ma’am.”

“Why?”

The question arrived so softly it barely sounded dangerous.

Jill searched for the answer that would cause the least damage.

“I don’t want problems here.”

“That is normal.”

Avery folded her hands.

“But you answer like someone taking an oral exam.”

Jill felt heat rise into her face.

Avery continued calmly.

“You monitor every sentence before you say it.”

Jill stayed silent.

“You apologize preemptively.”

Another pause.

“You also stop breathing normally whenever authority corrects you.”

Jill had not realized anyone could see that.

Avery watched her for another few seconds before continuing.

“I wanted to clarify something before this becomes institutionalized behavior.”

Jill looked up slightly.

Avery’s tone never changed.

“This facility is structured to create compliance. That is not the same thing as emotional collapse.”

The white noise machine hummed quietly near the wall.

Jill waited.

“If you make a mistake here,” Avery said, “the world does not end.”

That sentence should have sounded comforting.

Instead it frightened Jill more because it suggested Avery already believed Jill thought otherwise.

Avery finally opened the folder.

“Do you know why I called you back?”

“No ma’am.”

“I wanted to see whether you would appear more frightened the second time.”

Jill felt her stomach drop slightly.

Avery made one small notation.

“You did.”

Silence again.

Then Avery closed the file for good.

“That is all, Ms. Voss.”

Jill stood carefully.

At the door Avery spoke one final time.

“Doctor Carney measures outward behavior.”

Jill turned slightly.

“I measure internal strain.”

The counselor’s expression never changed.

“You should hope those two things stay aligned.”

Then Avery returned to her paperwork while Jill stepped quietly back into the corridor.
