
Pod One was called at 0745 hours.

The sixth-month offenders reacted before the announcement fully ended. Blankets folded. Sandals on. Hair secured. State-issued towels over shoulders. No hesitation anywhere in the pod.

They already knew the routine.

“Pod One. Medical processing. Shower rotation begins in five minutes.”

The women moved automatically toward the shower corridor in assigned order while Deputy Keller checked names against a clipboard. Nobody asked questions. Nobody spoke above a murmur.

By 0810 the first three offenders had already crossed the corridor to medical staging in pale blue hospital gowns and shower sandals.

The clinic suite stayed fixed in the east administrative wing. Two examination rooms. Intake tables outside. Portable privacy dividers. Stainless carts with sealed supplies. Four physician assistants moved offenders through vitals and intake while the two OB/GYNs remained inside the exam rooms continuously.

The pod cycled through in waves.

Shower.  
Gown.  
Staging.  
Exam.  
Post-shower.  
Uniform reissue.  
Return.

Efficient.  
Quiet.  
Practiced.

At 0958 Pod One returned to housing.

The women came back carrying folded replacement uniforms and small brown hygiene bags. Nobody discussed the visit. One offender climbed directly onto her bunk and rolled toward the wall. Another sat at a table silently brushing damp hair with slow careful strokes.

The first monthers in Pod Two watched all of it.

Nobody liked how quiet Pod One looked afterward.
