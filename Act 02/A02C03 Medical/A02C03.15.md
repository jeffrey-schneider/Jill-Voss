At 1215 hours the speaker activated again.

“Pod Two. Prepare for medical processing.”

The room changed instantly.

Not with discipline.

With nerves.

The first monthers stood too quickly, bumped tables, checked caps that no longer covered much hair after barber day. Jill saw three women immediately glance toward the whiteboard schedule even though the time had already been posted since breakfast.

Deputy Keller entered carrying two clipboards this time.

“Listen carefully. You will process in assigned order. No talking once movement begins. Shower rotations will be three offenders at a time.”

He looked directly at the line of black-and-white striped first monthers.

“If you create delays, medical stays later. If medical stays later, I stay later. I strongly advise against that.”

Nobody answered.

“Voss. Front.”

Jill stepped forward first.

“Jonson. Reed. Hernandez.”

The line slowly formed.

The newly shortened hair made the pod look different now. Less individual. Twelve nearly identical black-and-white striped figures standing beneath fluorescent lights with pale necks exposed where longer hair had been yesterday.

“Movement.”

The shower rotations began.

Three offenders disappeared behind the heavy shower-room door while the others waited along the wall with towels and folded gowns in their hands.

Steam drifted briefly into the pod each time the door opened.

Jill entered during the second rotation.

“Strip out completely,” a female officer instructed. “Place uniform in collection bin. Towel stays outside water area. You have six minutes.”

The showers themselves were institutional stainless fixtures set into concrete partitions. No curtains. No privacy beyond spacing.

Water thundered down around them.

Nobody spoke.

Jill washed quickly beneath water that was warmer than she expected. Around her the other first monthers moved with awkward efficiency, trying not to look at each other directly while simultaneously having nowhere else to look.

“Thirty seconds,” the officer called.

Water shut off automatically moments later.

The women dried quickly and stepped into pale blue gowns issued from a rolling cart outside the shower room. Thin material. Open-backed. Ties at neck and waist.

Jill felt colder immediately.

“Line up.”

The movement to medical staging felt longer than the haircut march had.

At the entrance to the clinic corridor, a PA checked names and attached temporary paper wristbands.

“Any chance of pregnancy?”

“No ma’am.”

“Current medications?”

Jill answered automatically while another PA recorded blood pressure and temperature at the next station.

Everything moved continuously.

Nobody sat long enough to relax.

From farther inside the suite Jill could hear:  
drawer slides,  
rolling stool wheels,  
paper crinkling on exam tables,  
quiet professional voices,  
running sink water.

The clinic smelled sharply clean compared to the rest of the facility.

One offender exited Exam Room Two with damp hair and reddened eyes but a completely blank expression. A PA guided her toward post-processing showers without comment.

“Voss.”

Jill looked up.

“Exam Room One.”

The PA held the door open.

Inside, the OB/GYN did not look up immediately from the chart.

“Ms. Voss. Have a seat.”

Outside the room, Pod Two continued moving through the system one offender at a time.
