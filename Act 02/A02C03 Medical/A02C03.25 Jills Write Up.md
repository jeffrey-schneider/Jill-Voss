Evening count had already cleared when Deputy Martin entered Pod Two carrying the brown discipline folder beneath one arm.

The pod lights were dimmed to nighttime level again. Outside the narrow upper windows the Oklahoma sky had finally gone dark for real this time.

Most of the offenders were already in their bunks.

Martin stopped near the officer station and opened the folder.

“Voss.”

Jill sat up immediately from the lower bunk.

“Front.”

“Yes ma’am.”

She crossed the pod quietly in striped pajamas and shower sandals while the rest of Pod Two pretended not to watch.

Martin removed a single sheet from the folder.

“Medical movement notation. Sign receipt.”

Jill accepted the paper carefully.

The form was smaller than she expected.

Facility heading across the top.  
Date.  
Time.  
Location.

Then:

> OFFENDER FAILED TO MAINTAIN ISSUED GOWN CONTROL DURING MEDICAL MOVEMENT AFTER VERBAL INSTRUCTION.

Reporting Supervisor:  
T. Carney

Jill felt her stomach sink.

The violation category beneath it read:

> MINOR CONDUCT — DOCUMENTED COUNSELING

No punishment listed.

That somehow made it worse.

Martin handed her a pen.

“Signature only acknowledges receipt. Not agreement.”

Jill signed.

Martin took the form back, checked the signature line, then slid the paper neatly back into the folder.

“You’re collecting paperwork pretty fast for a first week.”

“Yes ma’am.”

Martin studied her for another second.

“Word of advice?”

“Yes ma’am.”

“Doctor Carney notices patterns.”

The folder closed with a soft snap.

“Return to bunk.”

“Yes ma’am.”

Jill climbed back into bed while Martin continued count verification farther down the pod.

Around her the room settled gradually into nighttime sounds.

Sheets moving.  
Quiet coughing.  
The hum of ventilation.  
An occasional toilet flushing behind partial privacy walls.

Nobody spoke to her.

That almost felt deliberate now.

Jill rolled toward the concrete wall and stared at the dark seam where the mattress met the block.

Minor conduct.

Documented counseling.

No punishment listed.

Yet.

That part kept returning.

The institution seemed to work in layers. Nothing dramatic happened immediately. Things were written down. Logged. Remembered. Then one day the consequences appeared already fully formed.

Hair.

Gown.

Movement.

Corrections.

Patterns.

She could still hear Carney’s voice clearly:

> “Medical processing does not suspend standards.”

Jill pulled the thin blanket higher around herself.

Somewhere across the pod an offender began quietly crying into a pillow. Not loudly enough for staff intervention. Just enough to hear if the room stayed silent long enough.

Nobody acknowledged it.

Jill closed her eyes.

For almost an hour she kept imagining files growing thicker.
