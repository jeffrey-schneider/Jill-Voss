Morning routine had already started by the time the mental-health counselor entered Pod Two.

Breakfast trays were gone. Bunks had been tightened for inspection. Several offenders sat at the tables beneath the television pretending to watch a daytime court show while others wiped sinks or folded state blankets with careful precision.

The pod door buzzed open.

Conversations lowered automatically.

The woman who entered did not wear a custody uniform. No badge clipped to a tactical vest. No radio belt heavy with restraints and keys.

Dark cardigan.  
Clipboard.  
State identification lanyard.

Counselor Avery.

Jill recognized her immediately from intake week.

Back then Avery had spoken softly in a small processing office while offenders answered mental-health screening questions beneath fluorescent lights and security cameras.

History of depression.  
Self-harm.  
Medication use.  
Panic attacks.  
Trauma history.

At the time Jill assumed she would never see her again.

Deputy Keller looked up from the officer station.

“Morning.”

“Morning, Deputy.”

Avery scanned the pod quietly for several seconds before checking names on the clipboard.

Unlike custody staff, she did not bark orders across the room. Somehow that made everybody pay closer attention.

“Reed, Marla.”

Marla immediately looked tense.

“Counseling room after morning cleanup, please.”

“Yes ma’am.”

Avery wrote something down.

“Jonson, Cam.”

Cam straightened slightly at the table.

“Yes ma’am?”

“I’d like a follow-up conversation today.”

Cam nodded once.

Jill noticed several offenders carefully not looking at either woman now.

Mental-health pullouts carried their own rumors inside institutions.

Avery continued reviewing the pod.

“Voss, Jill Margaret.”

Jill looked up immediately.

“Yes ma’am.”

“I’d like ten minutes with you this morning as well.”

“Yes ma’am.”

Avery marked the clipboard again.

No explanation was given.

No one asked for one.

That was another thing Jill was beginning to understand about the facility. People disappeared into offices constantly:  
medical,  
classification,  
counseling,  
administration.

Then they returned quieter.

Avery closed the clipboard.

“The rest of you continue normal routine.”

She turned toward Keller near the officer station.

“Do you have availability after inspection?”

Keller checked the movement board.

“You can have interview room two from 0930 until noon.”

“Perfect.”

Avery thanked him and started toward the exit before pausing briefly near Jill’s table.

“Ms. Voss.”

“Yes ma’am?”

“Bring your state folder with you.”

Jill felt her stomach tighten again.

“Yes ma’am.”

The counselor left the pod.

The heavy door locked behind her with a metallic click.

Almost immediately the room’s background noise slowly resumed, though quieter than before.

Across the table, one offender muttered under her breath:

“They never come in here for good news.”

Nobody answered her.
