The door closed softly behind her.

Exam Room One looked smaller than Jill expected.

Bright.  
Cold.  
Organized.

A padded examination table occupied the center wall beneath an adjustable light. Stainless trays stood nearby holding sealed instruments and labeled supply packets. A rolling stool rested beside a counter-mounted computer terminal.

The OB/GYN continued reviewing the chart for another few seconds before finally looking up.

“Jill Voss?”

“Yes ma’am.”

“I’m Doctor Levin.”

The doctor’s tone was calm and practiced. Not warm. Not unfriendly. Professional in the way people became when repeating the same process hundreds of times.

Doctor Levin glanced briefly toward the chair beside the exam table.

“Sit.”

Jill obeyed carefully, trying not to let the thin hospital gown shift open behind her.

The doctor typed several notes.

“First month?”

“Yes ma’am.”

“Any current medications?”

Jill answered.

“History of surgeries?”

“No ma’am.”

“History of abnormal exams, reproductive conditions, or current concerns?”

“No ma’am.”

Doctor Levin nodded once and entered the responses rapidly into the chart. She did not waste movement. Every question arrived in the same measured cadence.

Outside the room Jill could faintly hear movement continuing in the corridor. A cart wheel squeaked briefly. Somebody called for fresh gowns. Water pipes hummed somewhere beyond the wall.

The facility kept moving.

Doctor Levin finished typing.

“Alright. This will be a routine women’s wellness screening. If at any point you have questions, ask them. Understood?”

“Yes ma’am.”

The doctor put on gloves.

“Step to the exam platform.”

Jill stood.

The paper covering the padded table crackled softly beneath her as she climbed up and positioned herself where directed.

“Further back.”

Jill shifted.

“A little more.”

The doctor adjusted the overhead light.

The examination itself remained clinical and efficient. Instructions delivered quietly. Movements controlled. The doctor narrated only what was necessary.

“Relax your knees.”

“Small pressure.”

“Deep breath.”

Nothing in Doctor Levin’s demeanor suggested humiliation, embarrassment, or punishment. To her, this was simply scheduled medical processing inside a correctional facility.

That almost made it stranger.

Jill stared upward at the ceiling tiles while the examination continued. Somewhere beyond the door another offender was being checked into staging.

The world outside the room continued operating normally while she lay beneath fluorescent lights in a paper-lined room wearing a thin institutional gown with yesterday’s regulation haircut still rough against the back of her neck.

Doctor Levin finished one portion of the exam and removed her gloves.

“Any pain?”

“No ma’am.”

“Any concerns you have not reported?”

“No ma’am.”

The doctor studied her briefly then nodded once.

“You appear healthy.”

The sentence should have sounded reassuring.

Inside the institution it sounded more like classification.

Doctor Levin removed another pair of gloves from the box.

“One final portion of the exam and we’ll have you routed back through processing.”

Jill swallowed once.

“Yes ma’am.”

Outside, the corridor speaker buzzed faintly.

“Pod Two. Maintain movement.”

The clinic continued operating around her like machinery.
