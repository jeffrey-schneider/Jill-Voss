Interview Room Two was smaller than Jill expected.

Not cold exactly.

Controlled.

Soft overhead lighting replaced the harsher fluorescent fixtures used in the pod. A state-issued landscape print hung on one wall beside a white noise machine that hummed quietly near the door. Two padded chairs faced each other across a narrow laminate table.

Counselor Avery sat waiting with Jill’s folder already open.

“Come in, Ms. Voss.”

Jill stepped inside.

The door closed behind her with a magnetic click.

Avery motioned toward the chair.

“Sit wherever you’re comfortable.”

That almost made Jill laugh.

There was only one chair.

Jill sat carefully and placed her state folder on the table where instructed.

Avery thanked her softly and added it to the stack already open before her. Several clipped forms sat visible for only a second before Avery turned them slightly away.

Jill still recognized Dr. Carney’s notation sheet.

Avery noticed the glance.

“Relax,” she said calmly. “I’m not disciplinary.”

The answer arrived too quickly to feel reassuring.

Avery folded her hands neatly atop the file.

“How are you sleeping?”

“Fine, ma’am.”

“You answered that very quickly.”

Jill stayed quiet.

Avery did not press immediately. She simply made a small note on the page in front of her.

The scratching of the pen sounded louder in the quiet room.

“You’ve accumulated several corrections in a short amount of time.”

“Yes ma’am.”

“Do you feel staff are targeting you?”

Jill hesitated.

“I don’t think so, ma’am.”

“You paused.”

“I was trying to answer correctly.”

Avery nodded once as though the distinction mattered.

“That’s interesting phrasing.”

Jill felt herself sit straighter.

Avery continued calmly.

“Are you afraid of making mistakes here?”

“Yes ma’am.”

“More afraid of the punishment or of the attention?”

Jill opened her mouth slightly, then stopped.

Avery waited.

That was the difficult part. She never rescued silence. She simply let it sit between them until Jill felt pressure to fill it.

“I don’t know,” Jill admitted finally.

Another note.

“Have you noticed yourself changing behavior to avoid documentation?”

“Yes ma’am.”

“How?”

Jill looked briefly toward the wall.

“I think before I move now.”

Avery nodded again.

“That is usually the point of structure.”

The answer could have been therapeutic.

Or institutional.

Jill could not tell which.

Avery turned one page in the file.

“Tell me about the haircut situation.”

Jill explained quietly. The caps. The warnings. Dr. Carney. The barber detail.

Avery listened without interruption.

When Jill finished, Avery asked:

“Did you believe the consequence was unfair?”

“No ma’am.”

“Did you believe it was excessive?”

Jill hesitated too long again.

Avery watched her carefully.

“You can answer honestly here.”

That sentence increased Jill’s stress immediately.

Honesty suddenly felt dangerous.

“I thought maybe the whole pod shouldn’t have been punished because of me.”

Avery leaned back slightly.

“And how did the pod respond to you afterward?”

“There wasn’t really yelling or anything.”

“That wasn’t my question.”

Jill lowered her eyes briefly.

“…they got quieter.”

Avery wrote something else down.

“You are socially observant.”

The statement landed oddly. Not compliment. Not warning.

Observation.

Avery closed the folder partway.

“One more question, Ms. Voss.”

“Yes ma’am.”

“What do you think happens to offenders who become emotionally isolated during first month?”

Jill felt her stomach tighten again.

“I don’t know, ma’am.”

Avery held her gaze calmly.

“That answer concerns me more than if you had guessed.”

Silence settled across the room again.

Then Avery closed the file completely.

“We’ll speak again next week.”

Not:  
if needed.

Not:  
if problems continue.

Just:  
next week.

Avery stood.

“You may return to the pod.”

Jill rose carefully.

As she reached the door Avery spoke once more without looking up from the paperwork.

“And Ms. Voss?”

“Yes ma’am?”

“Try not to confuse compliance with adjustment. They are not the same thing.”

Jill left the room less certain of herself than when she entered.
